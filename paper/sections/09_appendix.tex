\subsection{Verification of the corrected implementation}
\label{app:verification}

The claim that \Gath{} and \Mask{} compute the same function while executing
different work is load-bearing, so it is pinned by regression tests rather than
asserted:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Functional equivalence.} Maximum absolute deviation between the
    two paths is $<10^{-10}$ in \texttt{float64} for
    $N \in \{6,12,24\}$ and $k \in \{1,2,3,N-1\}$.
  \item \textbf{Distinct executed work.} With
    $N{=}48, k{=}4, B{=}8, d{=}16$ and input width $32$, a FLOP counter reports
    $2{,}555{,}904$ for dense and \Mask{} --- identical, because the counter
    scores matrix products and \Mask{} issues the same two as dense --- against
    $2{,}015{,}232$ for \Gath{}. The difference, $540{,}672$, is exactly the
    aggregation term $2BNd(N-k)$: two FLOPs per multiply--add, which is the
    counter's convention rather than the MAC convention of
    Section~\ref{sec:bound}, and the factor cancels in every ratio. \Mask{}'s
    selection, masking and renormalisation are elementwise and are not matrix
    products, so they add executed work that this counter does not see; the
    equality above is therefore an equality of aggregation MACs, not of total
    executed work.
  \item \textbf{No dense aggregation in the sparse path.} Tracing every
    \texttt{torch.matmul} confirms \Gath{} issues no
    $(B,N,N)\times(B,N,d)$ product and does issue
    $(B,N,1,k)\times(B,N,k,d)$.
  \item \textbf{The score matmul survives in all modes.} Every top-$k$ variant
    still issues the full $(B,N,d)\times(B,d,N)$ product, which is
    assumption A2 of Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} made mechanical.
  \item \textbf{The ceiling holds numerically.} For
    $N \in \{6,\dots,512\}$ the ratio $C_\text{dense}/C_{\text{top-}k}$ is
    verified $<2$, and the structural variant of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:escape} is verified to track $N/k$ in the
    zero-selector-cost case, $C_{\mathrm{sel}} = 0$, which is the only case a
    pure operation count can pin: a nonzero $C_{\mathrm{sel}}$ moves the ratio
    below $N/k$ by exactly the displayed formula.
  \item \textbf{Selection order is irrelevant.} \texttt{topk} with
    \texttt{sorted=False}, which the gathered path uses, agrees with the
    sorted call to $5.4\times10^{-7}$ in \texttt{fp32} at every $(N,k)$ tested,
    confirming that the softmax and gather are permuted consistently.
  \item \textbf{Which backend serves the fused arm.} A probe forces each SDPA
    backend in turn, at both dtypes and at $N \in \{12,192,512\}$, under no
    mask, an additive float mask and a Boolean mask, and the profiler records
    the kernel the default dispatcher actually launches. FlashAttention is
    rejected under any non-null mask, in both the additive-float and the
    Boolean form, and in \texttt{fp32} under any input; the fused arm runs
    \texttt{fmha\_cutlassF}, PyTorch's memory-efficient CUTLASS kernel, at both
    dtypes. On the same unmasked \texttt{bf16} shape, forcing FlashAttention
    rather than the memory-efficient backend gives $0.0149$ against
    $0.0223$\,ms at $N{=}192$ and $0.0388$ against $0.0989$\,ms at $N{=}512$,
    so a baseline able to reach FlashAttention would widen the fused gap we
    report rather than narrow it. The log is released.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The CPU allocator control}
\label{app:cpu}

The CPU numbers of Section~\ref{sec:cpu} required pinning the heap before they
measured attention rather than the allocator. Dense, masked and gathered each
allocate and free several multi-MiB $(B,N,N)$ temporaries per forward; the
default glibc allocator returns those pages to the kernel, and they are
re-faulted on the next call. We raise glibc's \texttt{MALLOC\_TOP\_PAD\_} so
that freed temporaries are retained instead, and count minor faults per forward
from the process's own resource usage across the timed region.

Because the tuning is part of the measurement rather than a detail of it, the
harness records the allocator environment, the thread counts, the affinity mask
and the fault totals in each run's metadata, and the superseded un-pinned run
is retained beside the pinned one. The effect it removes is large and unequal
across arms: at $N{=}192$ the un-pinned run takes $\approx37{,}000$ extra minor
faults per forward and inflates latency by up to $8.8\times$, and at $N{=}48$
it inverted the dense/gathered ordering --- gathered appeared $0.81\times$
unfused dense un-pinned, against $3.13\times$ once the fault storm is removed.
That was the only cell in this study, on either device, where a gathered path
appeared to beat a dense one on equal terms.

\subsection{Pre-registration chronology}
\label{app:prereg}

The analysis plan of Section~\ref{sec:training} was committed when $8$ of the
$40$ tier-2--4 runs had produced a final evaluation --- all five seeds of
\texttt{gathered-}$k{=}2$ and three of five of \texttt{random-}$k{=}2$ --- and
before any tier-2--4 return had been inspected. Commit history establishes the
ordering: tier-1 artifacts at 12:18, the plan at 12:56, tier-2--4 artifacts at
19:11 the same day. The tier-1 arms were complete and had been inspected, which
is why the tier-1 gate is excluded from the inferential family.

The pre-registration document has two commits: the plan itself, and a later
append to its deviations log. The diff between them touches only the deviations
section; the endpoint, contrasts, test, effect sizes, interval and correction
are byte-identical to the pre-unblinding version. The log records one item, the
bootstrap/permutation tension discussed in Section~\ref{sec:training}.
Timestamps establish ordering and nothing more --- they cannot establish that a
file on disk was never opened --- so the plan's independence is stated as a
claim about our conduct rather than as something the artifact proves, and data
generation had demonstrably already begun.

\subsection{The reporting-audit procedure}
\label{app:audit}

For each of the twelve papers in Table~\ref{tab:audit} we read the full text,
including appendices and the official supplement where one exists, searched it
for \emph{latency}, \emph{runtime}, \emph{time}, \emph{throughput},
\emph{speed}, \emph{wall-clock} and \emph{FLOP/s}, and recorded (i) whether it
makes a computational-efficiency or complexity claim about its mechanism, (ii)
whether it reports any measured temporal quantity attributable to that
mechanism, and (iii) the figure, table or sentence that settled the decision. A
statement such as ``training took a few days'' does not qualify, since it is
not attributable to the mechanism. The completed sheet is released with the
artifacts, because a ``no'' is an absence claim and an absence claim is only as
good as the search behind it. Scope matters for at least one entry: MAT's
``yes'' rests on a wall-clock figure in its appendix and would be a ``no'' on
the main body alone.

\subsection{Secondary training analysis}
\label{app:training}

\paragraph{Two results we report but do not interpret.} C4 has the one sign
opposite to its pre-registered direction, and should be set aside for a reason
independent of its $p$-value: neither $N{=}12$ arm learned the task. A
uniform-random policy scores $-758.9$ under the same evaluation protocol,
against $-864.9$ for the dense arm --- worse than random, two of five seeds
below $-975$ --- and $-717.9$ for the gathered arm, so the $+147$ point estimate
compares a diverged run with a barely learning one. Second, for C4 and C6 the
bootstrap interval excludes zero while the permutation test does not. The
pre-registration named the permutation test, so it governs; the disagreement is
unsurprising at $n{=}5$, where only $5^5 = 3125$ distinct resamples exist per
arm and the interval is coarse, discrete and of uncertain coverage. We treat it
as descriptive and keep both on the page, so the reader sees the size of the
estimated effect as well as the strength of the evidence for it.

Descriptively, and supporting no claim: the Lagrangian arm meets its budget but
not by the intended route, its gate collapsing to expected $k \approx 1.01$
within the first $8\%$ of training while the dual multiplier decays to zero;
entmax settles at a support of $1.64$ of $5$ peers; and attention entropy falls
from $0.35$ nats over $5$ peers to $0.12$ over $2$. The three sit on different
supports and are reported only as an instrumentation check.

\subsection{The partially observable environment}
\label{app:env}

The variant used in Section~\ref{sec:training} modifies \texttt{simple\_spread}
in one respect: for each agent $i$ and peer $j$, the relative-position entry
$\text{peer\_rel}[i,j]$ is zeroed when
$\lVert \text{peer\_rel}[i,j] \rVert_2 > R$. Reward, action space, landmark
observations, episode length and observation dimension are unchanged, and
$R{=}1.0$ throughout.

Two properties of the variant should be stated because they bound what it can
support. First, the mask is a zeroing rather than an explicit visibility flag,
so a peer at relative position exactly $(0,0)$ is not distinguishable from an
absent one; measured under a uniform random policy, $0.06\%$ of visible peers
at $N{=}6$ and $0.15\%$ at $N{=}12$ lie within $0.05$ of the observer, which
bounds how often the ambiguity can arise. Second, the visible \emph{fraction}
falls with $N$ while the absolute number visible rises, because the world size
is fixed; the two series are given in Section~\ref{sec:training}, and the
consequence is a difficulty confound that rules out across-$N$ return
comparisons in this environment.

The communicating arms carry between $7.2\%$ ($N{=}12$) and $19.0\%$
(\texttt{simple\_reference}) more trainable parameters than the
communication-free MAPPO baseline, since the attention module adds parameters
that the baseline has no analogue for. The positive controls therefore
establish that the communicating architecture outperforms the baseline, not
that the channel alone accounts for the difference. Within the pre-registered
family the arms share an architecture and the comparison is tighter; C1 in
particular holds $k$, executed FLOPs and every hyperparameter fixed.

\subsection{Reproduction and provenance}
\label{app:repro}

The measurement campaign is training-free and completes in minutes. The
released harness regenerates the tables in this paper from the raw JSON
artifacts, and the training arms are driven by generated configuration files
with the tier ordering enforced by the runner.

Two provenance caveats. The aggregate provenance record covers the earlier
microbenchmark phases of this project, which ran on different hardware that is
no longer available; run-level provenance for the training arms is in the
per-run configuration files rather than in that record. The CPU numbers in Section~\ref{sec:cpu} were taken with the glibc heap
pinned; an earlier campaign that did not do so is superseded and retained in the
artifacts with a note. The activation-memory measurement is reported from a run
with the whole card free, since a shared-host repeat exhausts memory one $N$
earlier for the gate; the fitted exponents for the other four arms are identical
between the two runs.
