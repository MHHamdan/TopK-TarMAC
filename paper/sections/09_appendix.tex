\subsection{Verification of the corrected implementation}
\label{app:verification}

The claim that \Gath{} and \Mask{} compute the same function while executing
different work is load-bearing, so it is pinned by regression tests rather than
asserted:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Functional equivalence.} Maximum absolute deviation between the
    two paths is $<10^{-10}$ in \texttt{float64} for
    $N \in \{6,12,24\}$ and $k \in \{1,2,3,N-1\}$.
  \item \textbf{Distinct executed work.} With
    $N{=}48, k{=}4, B{=}8, d{=}16$ and input width $32$, a FLOP counter reports
    $2{,}555{,}904$ for dense and \Mask{} (identical), against $2{,}015{,}232$
    for \Gath{}. The difference, $540{,}672$, is exactly the aggregation term
    $2BNd(N-k)$.
  \item \textbf{No dense aggregation in the sparse path.} Tracing every
    \texttt{torch.matmul} confirms \Gath{} issues no
    $(B,N,N)\times(B,N,d)$ product and does issue
    $(B,N,1,k)\times(B,N,k,d)$.
  \item \textbf{The score matmul survives in all modes.} Every top-$k$ variant
    still issues the full $(B,N,d)\times(B,d,N)$ product, which is
    assumption A2 of Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} made mechanical.
  \item \textbf{The ceiling holds numerically.} For
    $N \in \{6,\dots,512\}$ the ratio $C_\text{dense}/C_{\text{top-}k}$ is
    verified $<2$, and the structural variant of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:escape} is verified to track $N/k$.
  \item \textbf{Selection order is irrelevant.} \texttt{topk} with
    \texttt{sorted=False} agrees with the sorted call to
    $5.4\times10^{-7}$ in \texttt{fp32} at every $(N,k)$ tested, confirming that
    the softmax and gather are permuted consistently.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The partially observable environment}
\label{app:env}

The variant used in Section~\ref{sec:training} modifies \texttt{simple\_spread}
in one respect: for each agent $i$ and peer $j$, the relative-position entry
$\text{peer\_rel}[i,j]$ is zeroed when
$\lVert \text{peer\_rel}[i,j] \rVert_2 > R$. Reward, action space, landmark
observations, episode length and observation dimension are unchanged, and
$R{=}1.0$ throughout.

Two properties of the variant should be stated because they bound what it can
support. First, the mask is a zeroing rather than an explicit visibility flag,
so a peer at relative position exactly $(0,0)$ is not distinguishable from an
absent one; measured under a uniform random policy, $0.06\%$ of visible peers
at $N{=}6$ and $0.15\%$ at $N{=}12$ lie within $0.05$ of the observer, which
bounds how often the ambiguity can arise. Second, the visible \emph{fraction}
falls with $N$ while the absolute number visible rises, because the world size
is fixed; the two series are given in Section~\ref{sec:training}, and the
consequence is a difficulty confound that rules out across-$N$ return
comparisons in this environment.

The communicating arms carry between $7.2\%$ ($N{=}12$) and $19.0\%$
(\texttt{simple\_reference}) more trainable parameters than the
communication-free MAPPO baseline, since the attention module adds parameters
that the baseline has no analogue for. The positive controls therefore
establish that the communicating architecture outperforms the baseline, not
that the channel alone accounts for the difference. Within the pre-registered
family the arms share an architecture and the comparison is tighter; C1 in
particular holds $k$, executed FLOPs and every hyperparameter fixed.

\subsection{Reproduction and provenance}
\label{app:repro}

The measurement campaign is training-free and completes in minutes. The
released harness regenerates the tables in this paper from the raw JSON
artifacts, and the training arms are driven by generated configuration files
with the tier ordering enforced by the runner.

The pre-registration document has two commits: the plan itself, and a later
append to its deviations log. The diff between them touches only the deviations
section; the endpoint, contrasts, test, effect sizes, interval and correction
are byte-identical to the pre-unblinding version. The log records one item, the
bootstrap/permutation tension discussed in Section~\ref{sec:training}.

Two provenance caveats. The aggregate provenance record covers the earlier
microbenchmark phases of this project, which ran on different hardware that is
no longer available; run-level provenance for the training arms is in the
per-run configuration files rather than in that record. And the CPU numbers in
Section~\ref{sec:cpu} were taken with the glibc heap pinned, which the archived
campaign did not do; the archived CPU series is superseded for that reason and
is retained in the artifacts with a note.
