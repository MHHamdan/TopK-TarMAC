\paragraph{What the claim covers.} Every cost result here concerns
centralised-inference computational cost: the wall-clock latency and memory of
executing the communication module on a single device, where all agents'
observations are available to one process. It is not a claim about
bandwidth-constrained distributed deployment. In that regime the objective is
to reduce the number of messages physically transmitted, and top-$k$
sparsification may well be the correct design even though it does not reduce
local compute. The analytic ceiling of Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} says
nothing about transmitted bytes.

\paragraph{Measurement envelope.} Single-round attention; scaled dot-product
attention with the fused backends available in PyTorch 2.11 / CUDA 12.8; input
embedding width $64$; head width $d \in \{16,32,64,128,256\}$; $N \le 512$;
batch $32$--$4096$; \texttt{fp32} and \texttt{bf16}; eager,
\texttt{torch.compile} and CUDA-graph execution. Hardware: one GPU architecture
(NVIDIA Blackwell, sm\_120) and one x86\_64 CPU. We make no claim outside this
envelope. Within it, the head-width trend is unresolved: at $N{=}512$ the ratio
trends downward in $d$ without being strictly monotone, and the sweep ends at
$d{=}256$, so we do not know where, or whether, it reaches $1$ for larger
heads.

\paragraph{Our sparse implementation is not optimal, and we can bound by how
much.} \Gath{} is a straightforward PyTorch implementation using
\texttt{gather} and a batched contraction, not a hand-written fused kernel. We
removed one avoidable cost --- \texttt{torch.topk} sorts its output by default,
which is unnecessary here and was worth $5$--$24\%$ of the arm --- and one
remains: PyTorch's top-$k$ still runs at roughly $9\%$ copy-equivalent
bandwidth efficiency against the rate we measure, so a selection-optimal kernel
would recover a substantial part of the remaining selection term. Substituting
even a copy-equivalent select --- an optimistic counterfactual, since a real
selection kernel must also compare and rank --- into the
$N{=}512$ cell still leaves the gathered path near $3.4$\,ms against
$1.99$\,ms unfused and $0.43$\,ms fused, so the ordering survives; but a reader
should treat our ratios as upper bounds on the penalty, and a fully fused
selection--gather--contraction kernel remains the most informative single
follow-up to this work. Such a kernel could reduce intermediate traffic, launch
overhead and the cost of ranking. It could not avoid forming the score matrix,
which is assumption A2 and is what Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} rests on. We
do not claim that no implementation can ever cross over; we claim that none of
the ones available to us does, and that the analytic ceiling bounds what any of
them could win.

\paragraph{Generality across architectures.} The intensity argument of
Section~\ref{sec:roofline} predicts the same qualitative disadvantage wherever
the gathered path stays below the relevant machine balance. That argument is
device-independent by construction and therefore cannot itself be evidence of
device-independence. Our empirical support is one GPU architecture and one
CPU on which the ordering holds with ratios of the same order, plus a
same-device drift repeat establishing run-to-run stability. We were unable to replicate on a different GPU
generation --- the older hardware that produced this project's earlier runs is
no longer available. A reader who weights architectural replication heavily
should discount the generality accordingly. We also note that the CPU
comparison against the \emph{fused} baseline is dominated by cache blocking
rather than by arithmetic intensity, so it is weaker cross-architecture
evidence for the intensity mechanism than the unfused comparison is.

\paragraph{Two measurement effects that had to be controlled, and one that
remains.} Our CPU numbers required pinning the glibc heap; without it, page
faults on recycled $(B,N,N)$ temporaries inflate arms unequally and can invert
an ordering (Section~\ref{sec:cpu}). Our GPU ridge point reproduces to within
$2\%$, but the CPU one is measured with buffers smaller than the host's L3 and
is therefore a cache-bandwidth figure that does not reproduce: repeat
measurements of the same host give $1.35$ and $3.68$\,FLOP/byte, and a
DRAM-resident probe gives lower still. We use the CPU ridge only where the
conclusion holds across that whole range. What remains uncontrolled is that small-$N$ cells are
launch-bound, and their measured ratios depend on how many arms share a timing
group; two of our own sweeps disagree by $5\times$ on one such cell. Ratios at
$N \le 96$ should be read with that in mind.

\paragraph{Fused baseline.} Our fused arm passes a self-attention mask, and on
this build FlashAttention accepts no \texttt{attn\_mask} in any form, so all
fused numbers are PyTorch's memory-efficient CUTLASS kernel --- confirmed by
reading the dispatched kernel from the profiler, not inferred. Forcing
FlashAttention on the same unmasked shape is $1.5$--$2.5\times$ faster than
that kernel, so a baseline able to reach it would widen the gap we report
rather than narrow it.

\paragraph{Unlocked clocks.} We lacked the privilege to lock GPU clocks. We
compensate with interleaved timing, minimum-over-samples reporting, published
dispersion (Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}) and an explicit drift control
(Section~\ref{sec:drift}), and we report that absolute latency under concurrent
load can move by more than $100\%$. Our drift control is a same-device repeat;
it establishes stability across runs and hours, not independence from one
physical card, because a second card free of other tenants was not available
when these measurements were taken. Median ratio drift is roughly half the
median absolute-latency drift, though the worst loaded ratio cell moves as far
as the worst latency cell; all claims are stated in ratios, which buys
stability in the typical case rather than in the tail.

\paragraph{Statistical power of the training study.} Five seeds per arm gives a
minimum attainable two-sided permutation $p$ of $0.0079$ against a first Holm
threshold of $0.05/6 = 0.0083$ for six contrasts. The two are close enough that
a first rejection requires the smallest raw $p$ to sit at the discrete floor,
which for these untied data means one arm's five seeds lying entirely above the
other's; the family is not limited to one rejection --- Holm's later thresholds
are looser --- but no rejection at all is available on weaker evidence.
Resolving the $d \approx 0.6$ effect suggested by C1 would need roughly $45$
seeds per arm at two-sided $\alpha = 0.05$ and $80\%$ power, and roughly $70$
at the family-corrected $\alpha = 0.05/6$; both figures come from the standard
two-sample normal-approximation formula
$n = 2\bigl(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}\bigr)^2/d^2$, which is a guide to order
of magnitude rather than a power calculation for the exact permutation test we
actually run. We should be direct that this was a
design choice rather than a hard constraint: the entire $60$-run study consumed
$31.9$ GPU-hours against a $200$-hour guardrail, and a properly powered C1
would have cost about $37.8$. We allocated the budget to the measurement
campaign instead, which we would do again given that the computational result
is the paper's contribution --- but the six nulls are a consequence of that
allocation, not of an unaffordable experiment, and a reader is entitled to
weigh them accordingly.

\paragraph{Environment.} The partially observable \texttt{simple\_spread}
variant is our own, documented in Section~\ref{sec:training} and released. It
carries a difficulty confound across $N$ that we measure and publish, and which
is why no across-$N$ return comparison appears in this paper. Its zeroed
visibility mask does not distinguish an absent peer from one at the origin,
which affects $\le 0.15\%$ of visible peers in our runs. At $N{=}12$ neither
trained arm beats a uniform-random policy, so no contrast at that agent count
is interpretable (Section~\ref{sec:training}). Results on MPE tasks may not
transfer to environments with different observation structure, and our positive
controls establish that the communicating architecture helps rather than
isolating the channel, since the communicating arms carry $7$--$19\%$ more
parameters.
