Attention-based inter-agent communication aggregates over all $N-1$ peers per
agent per step, and restricting each agent to its top-$k$ peers by attention
score is an appealing way to make that cheaper. The case for it is normally
made by counting operations rather than by measuring time. We set out to build
such a mechanism and report why we abandoned it, at three levels.

First, an analytic ceiling. Because selecting the top-$k$ peers \emph{by
attention score} requires computing all $N$ scores, only the value-aggregation
matrix product is sparsifiable --- one of the two equal terms in an attention
multiply--accumulate count --- and the reduction is bounded above by
$2\times$ for any $k$ and any $N$, not the $1-k/(N-1)$ that the aggregation
term alone suggests. Sparsity whose pattern is fixed before any score is
computed escapes this bound, and reaches $N/k$ when its selector is cheap
relative to the sparse attention it enables; data-dependent top-$k$ cannot
escape it at all.

Second, what executes. Applying top-$k$ as a \emph{mask} on the dense attention
matrix, as our own first implementation did, leaves the dense aggregation
matmul in place, so the executed operation count is identical to dense while a
FLOP model based on $1-k/(N-1)$ reports a saving of $75\%$ at our headline
$N{=}512$, $k{=}127$ configuration and more as $k/N$ falls. We implement the
genuinely sparse gather, verify it computes the same function while executing
fewer operations, and benchmark dense, masked and gathered against a fused
baseline.

Third, what it costs. Across a head-width $\times$ batch $\times$ $N$ $\times$
$k$ sweep on one GPU architecture, the gathered path is slower than unfused
dense in all $46$ cells and slower than the fused kernel in $45$; the ordering
also holds on a CPU whose machine balance differs by more than an order of
magnitude, where it is $2.0$--$3.1\times$ slower than unfused dense. A component decomposition and a roofline analysis with measured
machine balance explain why: selection and gather move memory for no
arithmetic, the sparse contraction is slower than the dense matmul it replaces,
and at $N{=}512$ the sparse path moves $3.8\times$ the traffic for $0.6\times$
the arithmetic. Removing arithmetic from a memory-bound kernel does not save
time. We tune the sparse path where it is cheap to do so, and report what remains
on the table: its selection kernel still runs at under a tenth of the
copy-equivalent bandwidth the same device achieves elsewhere, and substituting
even a bandwidth-only counterfactual for it would not reverse the ordering.

A training study, whose analysis plan was registered after $8$ of $40$ runs had
completed and before any of their outcomes was read, establishes that the
communicating architecture improves return, so the sparsification contrasts are
not vacuous; it does not isolate the learned channel. Its six pre-registered
sparsification contrasts are under-powered nulls at five
seeds and are reported as such. We release the harness, the corrected
implementation, a checker that re-derives every headline number here from the
raw artifacts, and seven reproducible benchmarking pitfalls that produced
spurious results during this study.
