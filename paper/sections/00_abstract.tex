Attention-based inter-agent communication is the dominant scalability
bottleneck in cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL): every
realisation we surveyed pays an $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ message-aggregation
cost in the number of agents $N$. We propose \emph{TopK-TarMAC}, a
drop-in replacement for dense soft attention that aggregates only the
top-$k$ messages per agent, with $k$ adapted per agent per step by a
learned Gumbel-softmax gate trained end-to-end with the policy. On
cooperative \texttt{simple\_spread} (MPE) at $N \in \{3, 6, 12\}$ we
report three findings. (i) At $N{=}3$, attention communication does not
beat MAPPO — the cooperation problem is too small. (ii) At $N{=}6$,
\emph{dense} attention communication yields a Cohen's $d{=}1.06$
improvement over MAPPO with bootstrap CI $[+2.3, +26.5]$ on the mean
return delta. (iii) The adaptive-$k$ gate \emph{degrades as $N$ grows}:
at $N{=}12$, TopK-TarMAC underperforms MAPPO with $d{=}-1.87$ and
bootstrap CI $[-119.7, -28.5]$ that excludes 0 on the negative side. We
diagnose this honest negative result as gate-variance dominance — the
Gumbel-softmax over $N{-}1$ destinations introduces stochasticity that
scales with $N$ and overwhelms the FLOPs savings of 21--29\%. The paper
documents (a) the positive lever (dense attention helps at moderate $N$),
(b) the negative result for adaptive sparsification, and (c) the
diagnostic that motivates Lagrangian-budgeted gating as the natural next
step. Every number is regenerable from \texttt{bash scripts/reproduce.sh}
against a single tracked commit; the 64-paper literature review has zero
fabricated citations.
