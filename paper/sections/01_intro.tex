Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning systems increasingly pass
learned messages between agents, and attention is a widely used mechanism for
deciding what to listen to \citep{das2019tarmac,jiang2018atoc,jiang2018dgn}.
Because every agent attends to every other, the channel costs
$\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ per step, and a natural proposal follows: restrict each
agent to its top-$k$ peers by attention score, with $k \ll N$. The efficiency
case is easy to state --- if dense aggregation touches $N-1$ peers and sparse
aggregation touches $k$, a $1 - k/(N-1)$ fraction of the work disappears --- and
easy to accept, because it is an operation count and operation counts are cheap
to compute and hard to argue with.

This paper is a report on why we abandoned that design. We set out to build
score-based top-$k$ communication for multi-agent RL on exactly the rationale
above. Within the set of multi-agent communication papers we read for this work
and audit in Section~\ref{sec:related}, none selects peers by attention score
after computing all scores; the restriction
mechanisms there are largely structural, and, as we show below,
structural selection is not subject to the limit we derive. We state that as a
property of an audited set rather than of the literature, since an absence
claim over a literature would need a search protocol we did not run. The argument we
examine here is therefore one we made ourselves, and the implementation errors
we report are our own. We think the result is worth reporting anyway, because
the reasoning that led us there is standard, the failure has three distinct
causes at three levels of abstraction, and only the third is specific to our
hardware.

\paragraph{The count is smaller than it looks.} Selecting the top-$k$ peers by
attention score requires computing every score. The score matrix is therefore
irreducible, and only the value aggregation --- one of the two equal
multiply--accumulate terms in an attention operation count --- can be
sparsified at all. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} states this as a proposition: the
achievable reduction is bounded above by $2\times$, for
every $k$ and every $N$, approached only as $k/N \to 0$. The familiar
$1-k/(N-1)$ describes one of those two terms. The bound has a precise escape
route, which we make explicit because it is the design target for any successor
method: if the sparsity pattern is structural --- fixed by a topology, a spatial
prior, or a hash --- the score matmul becomes sparsifiable too, and the ratio
is no longer bounded by $2$. It reaches $N/k$ exactly when the selector's own
cost is negligible against the sparse attention it enables, which for a fixed
or precomputed pattern it is, and for a hash it is not automatically.

\paragraph{Even the reduced count need not be executed.} A count is a model of
cost, not a measurement of it, and an implementation can fail to perform the
saving the count describes. Applying top-$k$ as a \emph{mask} on the dense
attention matrix materialises the discarded entries as zeros and runs the same
dense $(B,N,N)\times(B,N,d)$ aggregation matmul; executed multiply--accumulates
are then identical to dense. This is what our own first implementation did, and
a FLOP model based on $1-k/(N-1)$ credits it with a saving that grows as $k/N$
falls: $75\%$ at our headline $N{=}512$, $k{=}127$ cell, and above $90\%$ at
the small-$k$ settings a learned gate tends towards. We implement
the genuinely sparse gather instead, verify it computes the same function to
floating-point tolerance while executing measurably fewer FLOPs, and benchmark
dense, masked and gathered as three separate curves against a fused dense
baseline.

\paragraph{And the executed saving need not be time.} The gathered
implementation is slower than unfused dense in all $46$ cells of a head-width
$\times$ batch $\times$ $N$ sweep, and slower than a fused kernel in $45$ of
them. Section~\ref{sec:measurement} decomposes why: the sparse contraction is
itself slower than the dense matmul it replaces, because a batched
matrix--vector product has far lower arithmetic intensity than a real GEMM;
selection and gather move substantial memory for no arithmetic; and at
$N{=}512$ the sparse path moves $3.8\times$ the traffic for $0.6\times$ the
arithmetic. Removing arithmetic from a memory-bound kernel does not make it
faster. We name the dense baseline and the envelope with every number, because
the two baselines differ by up to $4.6\times$ and which one is meant sometimes
changes the answer.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Analytical.} A $2\times$ ceiling on the operation-count saving
    achievable by any selection rule that requires the complete score vector,
    with its assumptions separated and
    its structural escape route stated as a constructive proposition that
    charges for the selector (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
  \item \textbf{Implementation.} A masked/gathered/fused taxonomy of the same
    function, a corrected sparse implementation, and regression tests pinning
    that the two top-$k$ variants agree numerically while executing different
    work (Sections~\ref{sec:analysis}, \ref{sec:measurement},
    Appendix~\ref{app:verification}).
  \item \textbf{Systems evidence.} A benchmark campaign over $N \le 512$, head
    width $16$--$256$, batch $32$--$4096$, two dtypes, three execution modes
    and two architectures, with a component decomposition and a roofline
    account of the cause, and a drift control quantifying how far the
    measurement travels (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}).
  \item \textbf{Learning evidence, bounded by its power.} A study with an
    analysis plan registered after $8$ of $40$ runs had completed and before
    any of their outcomes was read, separating ``the communication machinery
    does nothing'' from ``sparsifying it is free'', with positive controls that
    pass --- for the architecture, not for the channel in isolation --- and six
    sparsification contrasts reported as the under-powered nulls they are
    (Section~\ref{sec:training}).
  \item \textbf{Methodological artifacts.} Seven reproducible benchmarking
    pitfalls, each of which produced a spurious result during this study, and
    a released harness together with a checker that re-derives every headline
    quantity in this manuscript from the raw JSON and fails on any
    disagreement (Section~\ref{sec:pitfalls}).
\end{itemize}

Every claim concerns centralised-inference computational cost on one device,
not bandwidth-constrained distributed deployment, where the objective is to
reduce messages physically transmitted and where top-$k$ sparsity may well be
the right design. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states the full envelope,
including where our own implementation is known to leave performance on the
table.
