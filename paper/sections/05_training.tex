Sparsification could still be worthwhile if it cost nothing in return. Testing
that requires care, because on a task where the channel is not needed
``sparsification is free'' and ``the channel was never used'' make identical
predictions. We therefore gate the study on a positive control and
pre-register the analysis. The study is secondary to the computational result:
its role is to establish that the communicating architecture being sparsified
is doing something --- so that the sparsification contrasts are not vacuous ---
and to report what a five-seed design can and cannot resolve about return. It
does not isolate the learned attention channel, for reasons we give below.

\subsection{Design and pre-registration}

Twelve arms, $N \le 12$, five seeds, $2$M environment steps each, in four
priority tiers. All $N$-scaling is delegated to the training-free measurement
of Section~\ref{sec:measurement}, because end-to-end wall-clock in this
pipeline is environment-bound and cannot attribute a latency difference to the
module.

We use two communication-critical controls. In \texttt{simple\_reference} each
agent observes only its partner's goal, so the task requires information to
pass between agents; the environment provides its own discrete utterance
channel, and our control ablates the \emph{learned attention channel} on top of
it rather than removing communication altogether. It is fixed at $N{=}2$, where
attention entropy is identically zero, so it validates the message pathway
rather than the selection mechanism. To carry the $k$ arms we introduce a
partially observable \texttt{simple\_spread}: for agent $i$ and peer $j$, if
$\lVert \text{peer\_rel}[i,j]\rVert_2 > R$ that entry is zeroed, with $R{=}1.0$
and nothing else changed. Under a uniform random policy the mean fraction of
peers visible is $0.342, 0.307, 0.263, 0.235$ for $N = 3, 6, 9, 12$, while the
absolute number visible rises $0.68 \to 1.54 \to 2.10 \to 2.58$: the world does
not grow with $N$, so density rises and a fixed radius hides a different
fraction at each $N$. That is a difficulty confound across $N$, and it is why
no claim in this paper compares returns across $N$ in this environment.
Appendix~\ref{app:env} gives the remaining characterisation, including a
visibility aliasing rate below $0.15\%$ and a $7$--$19\%$ parameter-count
advantage for the communicating arms, which means the positive controls
establish that the communicating architecture helps rather than isolating the
channel.

The analysis plan was written and committed when $8$ of the $40$ tier-2--4 runs
had produced a final evaluation --- all five seeds of \texttt{gathered-}$k{=}2$
and three of five of \texttt{random-}$k{=}2$ --- and before any tier-2--4 return
was read. The tier-1 arms were complete and had been inspected, which is why
the tier-1 gate is excluded from the inferential family. Commit history
establishes the ordering (tier-1 artifacts 12:18, plan 12:56, tier-2--4
artifacts 19:11 the same day) and the plan was amended only by appending to its
deviations log. Timestamps establish ordering; they cannot establish that a
file on disk was unread, and we state the plan's independence as a claim about
our conduct rather than as something the artifact proves. The plan fixes the
endpoint (final greedy evaluation return, one number per seed), the six
contrasts and their direction, the test (exact two-sided permutation on the
difference of means over all $\binom{10}{5} = 252$ assignments), the effect
sizes, the interval (percentile bootstrap, flagged approximate at $n{=}5$), and
Holm--Bonferroni correction at FWER $0.05$. It also fixes the power expectation
in advance: the smallest attainable two-sided $p$ is $2/252 = 0.0079$ against
the first Holm threshold of $0.05/6 = 0.0083$. The margin between them is
$5\times10^{-4}$, so any rejection at all requires the smallest raw $p$ to sit
at the discrete floor, which for an untied difference of means means one arm's
five seeds lying entirely above the other's. Holm is a step-down procedure and
its later thresholds are looser, so further rejections remain possible once the
first is obtained; what the design forecloses is obtaining the first on
anything short of complete separation. Any null is
under-powered rather than evidence of absence, and no equivalence claim is
available because no TOST margin was pre-registered.

Table~\ref{tab:training} reports the outcome.

\paragraph{The communicating architecture helps.} Both positive controls
separate: $+4.26$ $[+3.15, +5.23]$ on \texttt{simple\_reference} and $+11.59$
$[+7.13, +16.44]$ on the partially observable task. Both attain $p = 2/252$,
the floor of a $5$ vs $5$ permutation test for this statistic on untied data,
which here is reached because all five seeds of one arm lie above all five of
the other. Measured attention
entropy on the $N{=}6$ control is $0.35$ nats against a $\ln 5 = 1.61$ ceiling,
so attention is peaked rather than uniform. What this establishes is that
sparsification is being applied to an architecture whose communication
machinery improves return under the evaluated setup --- the precondition that
makes the rest interpretable. It does not establish that the learned attention
channel alone accounts for the difference: the communicating arms carry
$7$--$19\%$ more trainable parameters (Appendix~\ref{app:env}), and
\texttt{simple\_reference} supplies an environment utterance channel of its
own, as described above. A
parameter-matched sham-communication arm would be needed for the
channel-specific causal claim, and we do not make it.

\paragraph{The six contrasts do not resolve a difference.} All six return
Holm-adjusted $p \ge 0.38$, the outcome the pre-registered power calculation
anticipated. We report this as a design that could not resolve effects of this
size, not as evidence that the effects are absent, and make no equivalence
claim. C1 is the contrast the design exists for: \texttt{gathered} and
\texttt{random-}$k$ are matched on $k$, on executed FLOPs and on every
hyperparameter, differing only in whether peers are chosen by attention score
or uniformly at random. Its point estimate favours attention ($+4.03$, HL
$+4.57$, $d = +0.60$) but does not approach significance. The design lacked
power to detect a selection effect of that size; nothing follows about whether
attention selects informatively.

\paragraph{Two results we report but do not interpret.} C4 has the one sign
opposite to its pre-registered direction, and it should be set aside for a
reason independent of its $p$-value: neither $N{=}12$ arm learned the task. A
uniform-random policy scores $-758.9$ under the same evaluation protocol,
against $-864.9$ for the dense arm --- worse than random, with two of five seeds
below $-975$ --- and $-717.9$ for the gathered arm. The $+147$ point estimate
compares a diverged run with a barely learning one, and we draw no conclusion
from it; the $N{=}12$ configuration needs a longer budget or a calibrated radius
before it can support any contrast. Second, for C4 and C6 the bootstrap
interval excludes zero while the permutation test does not. The
pre-registration named the permutation test, so it governs. The disagreement is
not surprising at this sample size: a percentile bootstrap of a mean at
$n{=}5$ resamples from a five-point plug-in distribution, and only $5^5 = 3125$
distinct resamples exist per arm, so the interval is coarse, discrete and of
uncertain coverage. We treat it as descriptive and let the pre-specified exact
permutation test govern inference, rather than asserting a systematic direction
to its miscoverage that our data cannot establish.
We keep both on the page so the reader sees the size of the estimated
effect as well as the strength of the evidence for it.

Descriptively, and supporting no claim: the Lagrangian arm meets its budget but
not by the intended route, its gate collapsing to expected $k \approx 1.01$
within the first $8\%$ of training while the dual multiplier decays to zero and
stays there; entmax settles at a support of $1.64$ of $5$ peers; and attention
entropy falls from $0.35$ nats over $5$ peers to $0.12$ over $2$, with $k{=}1$
forced to $0$ by construction, three numbers that sit on different supports and
are reported only as an instrumentation check.
