\subsection{Setup}
A cooperative Dec-POMDP is considered with $N$ agents, local observations
$o_i$, shared state $s$, joint action $a = (a_1, \ldots, a_N)$, and team
reward $r(s, a)$. Following MAPPO \citep{yu2022mappo}, a parameter-shared
actor $\pi_\theta(a_i \mid o_i, m_i)$ conditions on a message vector
$m_i$ produced by an inter-agent attention module, and a centralised
critic $V_\phi(s)$ is trained against the team-reward GAE return.

\subsection{Dense attention baseline (TarMAC-style)}
Each agent embeds its observation $e_i = \mathrm{GELU}(W_e o_i)$ and
emits a query/key/value triple $(q_i, k_i, v_i) = W_{qkv} e_i$. Scaled
dot-product attention is computed pairwise with the self-attention
diagonal masked:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(\langle q_i, k_j\rangle / \sqrt{d})\,[i\neq j]}
               {\sum_{j'\neq i} \exp(\langle q_i, k_{j'}\rangle/\sqrt{d})}.
\end{equation}
The dense channel aggregates over all peers,
$m_i^{\text{dense}} = W_o\,\mathrm{GELU}\!\left(\sum_{j\neq i} \alpha_{ij} v_j\right)$,
at a cost of $\mathcal{O}(N^2 d)$ per step for both the score matrix and
the aggregation.

\subsection{TopK-TarMAC}
The same soft attention scores $\alpha$ are computed, but only the $k$
highest-score entries per row are aggregated and renormalised:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{T}_i &= \mathrm{top}_k\!\left(\{\alpha_{ij}\}_{j\neq i}\right), \\
  \tilde{\alpha}_{ij} &= \frac{\alpha_{ij}\,[j \in \mathcal{T}_i]}
                       {\sum_{j'\in\mathcal{T}_i}\alpha_{ij'}}, \\
  m_i^{\text{topk}} &= W_o\,\mathrm{GELU}\!\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}_i}
                                                   \tilde\alpha_{ij} v_j\right).
\end{align}
Aggregation cost falls to $\mathcal{O}(N k d)$ per step; the score matrix
remains $\mathcal{O}(N^2 d)$ because top-$k$ selection requires the full
row.

\subsection{Adaptive $k$}
Rather than fixing $k$ globally, each agent chooses its own
$k_i \in \{1, \ldots, N{-}1\}$ from a small gate
$g_i = W_g e_i \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ sampled with Gumbel-softmax with
hard one-hot output:
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \mathrm{Gumbel\text{-}Softmax}(g_i,\, \tau, \, \text{hard}=\text{True}).
\end{equation}
At training time, gradients flow through the soft Gumbel via the
straight-through estimator; at evaluation time, $k_i = \arg\max p_i$ is
taken hard. Driving all $p_i$ to one-hot at $N{-}1$ recovers exact dense
attention, and zeroing the gate output recovers MAPPO with no
communication.

\paragraph{Why Gumbel-softmax, and what is known to break.}
Gumbel-softmax \citep{jang2017gumbel, maddison2017concrete} is the canonical
choice for differentiable discrete sampling and is the gate underlying the
sparsely-gated MoE \citep{shazeer2017moe} that Switch Transformers
\citep{fedus2022switch} later simplified to a hard top-1. Two known
failure modes are directly relevant to the on-policy MARL setting. The
straight-through gradient is biased: at evaluation time, gradients are
computed against the soft relaxation, not against the hard sample
returned to the policy, and the bias grows as the temperature is
annealed. Switch Transformers in particular report that router stability
requires bfloat16 storage with selective-precision FP32 routing and an
auxiliary load-balance loss; in the MARL setting, no auxiliary loss is
added (any preference for sparsity must emerge from the task signal
alone), and the policy-gradient variance further amplifies the bias.

\subsection{Objective and training}
The actor and critic are trained with MAPPO's clipped surrogate
\citep{yu2022mappo, schulman2017ppo}; no auxiliary loss is added on the
attention or gate output, so any preference for sparsity must emerge from
the task signal alone. No budget term on $k$ is added: the hypothesis
being tested is whether the policy itself prefers sparse communication,
not whether it can be constrained to.

\paragraph{Alternatives this design forgoes.} Three families of
differentiable sparsity would replace the sampled gate with a
deterministic one. Sparsemax \citep{martins2016sparsemax} and the
$\alpha$-entmax family \citep{peters2019entmax} project the attention
logits onto the simplex so that exact zeros arise from a transformation
with a closed-form Jacobian rather than from sampling. $L_0$
regularisation via hard-concrete gates \citep{louizos2018l0} penalises the
expected number of active connections directly. Constrained policy
optimisation \citep{achiam2017cpo}, and its dual-variable form with
proportional-integral-derivative control of the multiplier
\citep{stooke2020pidlagrangian}, would instead make the sparsity level an
explicit constraint. Each turns sparsity into a controlled input rather
than a quantity the policy gradient must discover through a stochastic
gate.
