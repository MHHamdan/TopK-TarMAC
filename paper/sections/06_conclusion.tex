We presented an honest empirical study of two questions in cooperative
MARL attention communication: \emph{does dense soft attention help?} and
\emph{can it be sparsified with a learned per-agent top-$k$ gate?}
The answer to the first, on \texttt{simple\_spread}, is yes at moderate
$N$: dense attention communication beats MAPPO at $N{=}6$ with Cohen's
$d{=}1.06$ and a 95\% bootstrap CI that excludes 0. The answer to the
second is \emph{not with the architecture we tested}: an end-to-end
Gumbel-softmax gate over the $N{-}1$ destinations introduces
seed-dependent variance that scales with $N$ and overwhelms the 21--29\%
FLOPs reduction it actually buys. At $N{=}12$, TopK-TarMAC is
significantly worse than MAPPO. The diagnostic --- between-seed std of
20--40 for adaptive-$k$ vs $\sim 5$ for dense and baseline --- points
to a clear next step: replace the unsupervised Gumbel gate with a
Lagrangian-budgeted constraint on $k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ or on the message
KL, so that sparsity is a controlled input rather than a noisy gradient.
The reduce-to-baseline switch we expose in the implementation makes that
experiment a one-flag change on top of the current code.
