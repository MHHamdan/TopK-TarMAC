Two questions in cooperative MARL attention communication were
investigated: whether dense soft attention helps, and whether the
attention channel can be sparsified with a learned per-agent top-$k$
gate. On \texttt{simple\_spread}, the first answer is positive at
moderate $N$: dense attention communication beats MAPPO at $N{=}6$ with
Cohen's $d{=}1.06$ and a 95\% bootstrap CI that excludes zero. The
second answer, for the end-to-end Gumbel-softmax architecture studied
here, is negative: gate-induced stochasticity scales with $N$ and
overwhelms the 21--29\% analytic FLOPs reduction it was reported to buy
---a reduction later found never to have been executed, and bounded above by
$2\times$ in principle (see the retraction in Section~\ref{sec:experiments});
at $N{=}12$,
adaptive-$k$ TopK is significantly worse than MAPPO. The diagnostic
signature --- between-seed standard deviation of 20--40 for adaptive-$k$
versus $\sim 5$ for dense and baseline --- points to a clear next step:
replace the unsupervised Gumbel gate with a budget-constrained
formulation in which sparsity is a controlled input rather than a noisy
gradient. The reduce-to-baseline switch exposed by the implementation
makes that experiment a one-flag change on the current code.
