\paragraph{Communication sparsity in multi-agent reinforcement learning.}
CommNet passes each agent the mean of the other agents' hidden states
\citep{sukhbaatar2016commnet}, TarMAC introduced signature-based attention
targeting \citep{das2019tarmac}, and MAT casts the joint policy as a sequence
model \citep{wen2022mat}. Once messages are weighted by attention, every
receiver scores every sender, and later methods restrict who communicates, some
of them for cost; DGN observes that receiving much information ``requires high
bandwidth and incurs high computational complexity'' \citep{jiang2018dgn}. The
computational arguments we found count the aggregation a restriction removes.
They do not count what the method spends deciding which peers to keep,
and that cost differs widely between methods, so we group them by when the
retained set is fixed and what fixing it costs, the axis of
Corollary~\ref{cor:taxonomy} (Section~\ref{sec:escape}). DGN's nearest
neighbours, ATOC's communication field and mean-field interaction fix the
retained set from structure available before any score exists
\citep{jiang2018dgn,jiang2018atoc,yang2018meanfield}. Such a selector is free if
the neighbourhood arrives with the environment and falls in case~(b) if it is
recomputed from all pairwise positions each step. IC3Net and SchedNet gate or
rank agents by a scalar per agent, a top-$k$ over an $O(N)$ vector instead of an
$N \times N$ matrix \citep{singh2018ic3net,kim2019schednet}. G2ANet and MAGIC
run a pairwise hard-attention or graph-construction network before
communication attention \citep{liu2020g2anet,niu2021magic}, so their selector
is of the same order as the scores they avoid. Score-then-select is the
limiting case, in which the selector is the score matrix itself. The adaptive
sparse communication graph of \citet{sun2020sparsecomm} applies a sparsemax
generalisation to logits already formed for every pair and motivates it partly
by reduced complexity, and handshake mechanisms that match a query against every
candidate key before selecting \citep{liu2020who2com} are score-then-select in
the same sense. Most of these methods are evaluated with two to roughly thirty
agents; DGN and ATOC reach about one hundred, and mean-field MARL, which
replaces pairwise interaction with an average over neighbours, one thousand.

\paragraph{Efficient and sparse attention.} Efficient-attention methods for long
sequences divide along the same axis. Sparse Transformers, Longformer and
BigBird fix the interaction pattern in advance, so the score computation itself
is sparse and the selector is essentially free
\citep{child2019sparse,beltagy2020longformer,zaheer2020bigbird}. Reformer and
Routing Transformer restrict candidates by hashing and online clustering, which
are content-dependent but evaluate scores only for candidate pairs, at a
selector cost that must be counted \citep{kitaev2020reformer,roy2021routing}.
Performer has no selector: it keeps every interaction and approximates the
softmax kernel so that the products can be reordered
\citep{choromanski2021performers}. Top-$k$ attention in sequence models is the
closest analogue of the mechanism we study. The Explicit Sparse Transformer
keeps the $k$ largest scores per query \citep{zhao2019explicit}, and
\citet{gupta2021topk} compute each query's exact top-$k$ over all keys in
chunks, with memory efficiency as the stated benefit. Sparsemax and
$\alpha$-entmax \citep{martins2016sparsemax,peters2019entmax} produce their
zeros after the scores exist, so by our reading they fall under
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}. That top-$k$ attention cannot approach $N/k$ has
been noted before. \citet{chen2023dfss} bound its speedup by a constant in a
memory-access model even with a mask supplied at no cost, and Block-Sparse
FlashAttention computes every query--key score to choose which value blocks to
skip, skipping about half the work of a pruned block and reporting kernel
speedups of up to $1.38\times$ \citep{ohayon2026bsfa}. Sparse mixture-of-experts
routing is also score-then-select \citep{shazeer2017moe,fedus2022switch}, and it
illustrates when the class pays: the router is small relative to the expert
computation it gates, so the analogue of the score term is minor and the saving
survives the selector. What decides the matter is the ratio between the cost of
scoring every candidate and the cost of the work skipped, and in single-head
attention that ratio is one.

\paragraph{Hardware-aware execution.} That operation counts do not determine
runtime is established in general terms \citep{dehghani2022misnomer}, and
transformer workloads are known to be dominated by data movement rather than
arithmetic \citep{ivanov2021datamovement}; the roofline model formalises when
arithmetic ceases to govern runtime \citep{williams2009roofline}. FlashAttention
and its successors reduce memory traffic without reducing the operation count
\citep{dao2022flashattention,dao2024flashattention2,shah2024flashattention3},
building on chunked computation that avoids materialising the score matrix
\citep{rabe2021selfattention}, and FlexAttention compiles attention variants
expressed as score modifications and block masks into fused kernels
\citep{dong2025flexattention}. These kernels raise the bar for any sparse
method, since the comparison that matters is against the fastest fused dense
kernel for the shape and format. Native Sparse Attention and MiniMax Sparse
Attention show where sparse attention does pay: blockwise selection amortises
the selector over blocks, preserves matrix-multiply shapes inside selected
tiles, and operates at $10^4$ to $10^6$ tokens, where the skipped work is large
\citep{yuan2025nsa,lai2026msa}. Communication among tens to hundreds of agents
with narrow heads offers neither block amortisation nor large skipped work, so
these results do not settle the multi-agent case.

\paragraph{Relation to this work.} The constant-factor limit of top-$k$ attention
was established by \citet{chen2023dfss} in a memory-access model and is visible
in Block-Sparse FlashAttention \citep{ohayon2026bsfa}. We build on it in two
ways: by making the cost of deciding an explicit term for every peer-selection
rule, so that score-then-select becomes one case among several, and by
measuring each case at the scale where multi-agent communication runs, tens to
hundreds of agents with narrow heads, against the fastest dense implementations we
could time for each numerical format, and on four GPUs. We follow the benchmarking
recommendations of \citet{hoefler2015benchmarking} where they apply, reporting
absolute latencies beside every ratio together with the statistic and its
run-to-run dispersion, and our kernels are written in Triton
\citep{tillet2019triton}.
