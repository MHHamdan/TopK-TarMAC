Every cost result here concerns centralised inference on a single device: the
latency and memory of executing the communication module in one process that
holds all agents' observations. It is not a claim about bandwidth-constrained
distributed deployment, where the objective is to reduce messages physically
transmitted and top-$k$ sparsification may well be correct even though it does
not reduce local computation. Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} says nothing about
transmitted bytes, and neither do the measurements. The analysis is also
single-round: assumptions A1 and A3 show that the operation-count \emph{ratio}
is unchanged by additional rounds or heads, but the measurements are of one
round of one head, and at the smaller agent counts we find cost governed by
kernel launches and cache residency, both of which multi-round communication
changes.

The measured envelope is one GPU architecture (NVIDIA Blackwell, sm\_120), one
x86\_64 CPU, PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 12.8 and Triton 3.6, scaled dot-product
attention through the fused backends that build provides, input embedding width
$64$, head width $d \in \{16,\dots,512\}$, $N \le 512$, batch $32$--$1024$,
\texttt{fp32} and \texttt{bf16}, and eager, compiled and CUDA-graph execution.
We were unable to replicate on a different GPU generation: the host has two
cards of the same model, and the older hardware that produced this project's
earlier runs is no longer available. The arithmetic-intensity argument of
Section~\ref{sec:roofline} predicts the same qualitative behaviour wherever the
relevant machine balance is similar, but an argument that is device-independent
by construction cannot itself establish device-independence, so generality
across GPU generations is untested. Both cards were shared with other tenants
throughout, which is why every claim is stated as a ratio between implementations
timed in the same interleaved sweep, and why the drift control is a same-device
repeat rather than a second card.

Our sparse implementations are not optimal and we can bound by how much in two
directions rather than one. The composed PyTorch path leaves substantial
performance on the table: removing an unnecessary sort was worth $5$--$24\%$,
and the remaining selection kernel runs at roughly $9\%$ of copy-equivalent
bandwidth. The fused kernels remove most of what remains, and they are the
reason the manuscript's earlier unconditional statement --- that the sparse path
is slower in every measured configuration --- does not survive. They are in turn
not the last word: they are written in Triton rather than tuned assembly, their
selection strategies are argmax rounds and threshold bisection rather than a
radix or bitonic select, they hold the whole score row in registers and so are
capped at $N \le 1024$, and they are forward-only, which is why the backward
comparison covers the composed implementations alone. A better kernel would
narrow the remaining gap further. What no kernel can remove is the requirement
to form and rank the full score matrix, which is assumption A2, and the
operation-count ceiling that follows from it.

The learning study is secondary and bounded by its power. Five seeds per
implementation gives a minimum attainable two-sided permutation $p$ of $0.0079$
against a first Holm threshold of $0.0083$, so any rejection in the
pre-registered family requires the smallest $p$ to sit at the discrete floor.
Resolving the $d \approx 0.6$ effect the primary contrast suggests would need
roughly $45$ seeds per implementation, or $70$ at the family-corrected level,
both from a normal-approximation formula that guides order of magnitude rather
than describing the exact permutation test we run. That was a budget allocation
rather than an unaffordable experiment: the whole $60$-run study consumed $31.9$
GPU-hours and a properly powered primary contrast would have cost about $37.9$
more. The positive controls establish that the communicating architecture
improves return rather than isolating the learned channel, since the
communicating variants carry $7$--$19\%$ more trainable parameters and one of
the two control tasks supplies a communication channel of its own; a
parameter-matched sham-communication variant would be needed for the
channel-specific causal claim, and we do not make it. Our partially observable
environment carries a difficulty confound across agent counts that we measure
and publish, which is why no across-$N$ return comparison appears, and its
centralised critic sees the same masked observations as the actors rather than a
privileged global state, which departs from standard practice and is a plausible
contributor to the failure of both variants to learn well at twelve agents.

Finally, the pre-score selectors of Section~\ref{sec:prescore} demonstrate a
cost structure, not a method. They attend to a different peer set than top-$k$
does, so the comparison against it is a comparison of cost and not of quality,
and we make no claim that spatial neighbours carry the information attention
would have selected. Whether a pre-score selector preserves task performance is
the obvious next question and one this paper does not answer.
