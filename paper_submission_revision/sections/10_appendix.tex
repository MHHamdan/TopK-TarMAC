\subsection{Dispersion behind the headline numbers}
\label{app:dispersion}

The primary statistic is the minimum over interleaved samples, on the modelling
assumption that contention can only add time. That statistic is trustworthy only
if the underlying sample was not wildly dispersed, so the harness records the
median, the interquartile range and the spread of per-run medians for every
configuration, and the released artifacts carry all of them. Two properties of
this host limit what any of it can mean. Both GPUs carried other tenants' allocations and near-continuous utilisation
throughout the campaign --- tens of gibibytes, varying between sessions, which
we observed but did not record per run --- and clocks could not be locked. We therefore report the spread of
per-run medians beside every configuration. Whether a bootstrap interval for a
ratio of minima is meaningful depends on the contention process, which is not
stationary on this host, so we do not report one for the minimum.

Two things follow for how the sweeps are read. The measurement reproduces well
enough that every ordering in this paper survives repetition, which is the
property the claims depend on. And the largest agent count is the least stable
one, not the most, so a margin of a few per cent there --- which is what the
end-to-end crossing of Section~\ref{sec:prescore} is --- is at the edge of what
this device can resolve, and we say so where we report it. We restrict the
crossover of Section~\ref{sec:fused} to $192$ agents and above for a different
reason: fixed per-call cost. A one-program Triton kernel that writes a single
float, timed the way every measurement here is timed, costs $1.8$\,\textmu s at
its minimum and $2.3$\,\textmu s at its median
(\texttt{scripts/launch\_overhead.py}). At $96$ agents the fastest dense
configuration is $13.8$\,\textmu s and the fastest fused sparse one
$11.7$\,\textmu s, so that fixed cost is an eighth of each, and the composed
sparse path issues several kernels rather than one. At $512$ agents the same
cost is one per cent of the dense measurement. A ratio between implementations
that issue different numbers of kernels is therefore partly a ratio of launch
counts at the small agent counts, and stops being one by $192$.

The pre-score sweep of Section~\ref{sec:prescore} is repeated in the same way,
and for a stronger reason: the crossing it reports is a margin of about ten per
cent rather than a factor, and a margin of that size has to be shown to survive
a second session before it can be reported at all. It does. The median movement
in the ratio between the two runs is $0.7\%$ and the worst is $8.5\%$; at the
agent count that carries the crossing the median is $0.8\%$ and the worst
$3.6\%$, and the four cells the crossing itself is read off agree to within
$1.5\%$. Unlike the core sweep, this one reproduces at every agent count it
measures, which is why the section quotes it from $48$ agents upward.

The repeat is on the same physical card, so it establishes stability across
runs and hours, not independence from one device, and we were not able to
obtain a second card free of other tenants. The tiling search of
Table~\ref{tab:autotune} was run twice, once on each card of the host. The two
searches select the same configuration at $63$ of the $72$ shapes they share,
and at head width $16$, where most of what this paper reports lives, at $16$ of
the $18$ shapes; the two disagreements are both sparse-kernel tilings. Their
absolute times differ by as much as $81\%$ with the contention each session
carried. That is why the search contributes a configuration and not a latency: every
sweep re-times that configuration in its own interleaved group, and the only
place a latency from the search's own session appears is
Table~\ref{tab:autotune}, whose columns are the search's own record and are
labelled as such. All measurements reported here
were taken on one of the two cards of this host, and no cross-device calibration
is claimed.

\section{Implementation and measurement validation}
\label{app:verification}

\subsection{Functional equivalence}

Every implementation is checked against the reference at every configuration
whose latency any released artifact reports: $158$ combinations of agent count,
head width and retained-peer count, $136$ of them with $k < N-1$ so that
selection is actually exercised, over $1.25$ million query rows in total. The
check matters more than it looks. Exact top-$k$ is discontinuous in the scores,
so two implementations that accumulate the score product in different orders can
rank a near-tied pair differently and aggregate over different peers, producing
a large output difference from a rounding-level score difference. An earlier
version of our per-query fused kernel computed the score row as a reduction
instead of through the matrix-multiply units and disagreed with the reference at
a handful of configurations for exactly that reason; the kernels reported here
use the same product as the reference.

With that fixed, every implementation agrees with the reference to
\texttt{fp32} rounding --- maximum deviation $3.2\times10^{-6}$, and zero for
the two implementations that share the reference's operator sequence exactly ---
with one attributable exception, which is an ambiguity in the function rather
than an error in an implementation. Exact top-$k$ is only well defined when the
$k$-th largest score is unique. The kernel that selects by threshold retains
every peer at or above that score, so a tie there gives it $k{+}1$ peers; the
kernel that sorts score--index pairs breaks the same tie by peer index, which is
not the order a rank-based selection uses. Four query rows in $1.25$ million
have such a tie, at three of the $158$ configurations, and those rows are the
only place any implementation differs from the reference by more than rounding.
Two further rows appear in the head-width sweep, at $192$ agents and head width
$128$, where the same two kernels differ from the reference by $1.7$ and $1.1$
on one query row of $6144$ at each of two retained-peer counts; the $k$-th score
on those rows is tied to the bit, so the divergence is the ambiguity and not an
error. The check is run by
\texttt{scripts/verify\_all\_configurations.py} and by the sweep harness
itself, both of which count how many query rows diverge as well as by how much,
and admit a tie as the explanation only when no more rows diverge than have a
tie.

The fused kernel for attention over a pre-chosen peer set is checked the same
way against the composed path it replaces, at every configuration of both pre-score sweeps and all three selectors --- $60$ configurations and $473{,}088$ query rows --- to a maximum deviation of $1.1\times10^{-6}$. In the fixed-set sweep of Section~\ref{sec:fused}, every top-$k$ path is checked on the timed inputs themselves, at all $48$ configurations: maximum deviation $2.4\times10^{-6}$ and no diverging row in $3.6$ million. All references are the composed fp32 paths; a float64 reference is used only in the precision study, where reduced-precision rounding changes the retained set. Its timed call also converts the
retained indices to the integer width the kernel reads them at, which the
composed path does not need, so the figure we report for it includes a cost the
comparison does not require of it.

\subsection{What each implementation executes}

The masked and gathered implementations compute the same function while
executing different work, which is the distinction the paper turns on, so
regression tests pin it. Maximum absolute deviation
between the two paths is below $10^{-10}$ in \texttt{float64}. A FLOP counter
attached to the running program reports identical totals for dense and masked
--- the counter scores matrix products, and masked issues the same two as dense
--- against a smaller total for gathered, the difference being exactly the
aggregation term. Tracing every matrix product confirms that the gathered path
issues no dense aggregation product and does issue the compacted one, and that
every top-$k$ variant still issues the full score product, which is assumption
A2 made mechanical.

The four hand-written kernels differ only in how the retained set is reached,
and each one is stated here in the terms Section~\ref{sec:measurement} compares
them in. All four compute the score row through the same matrix-multiply
sequence as the reference, in \texttt{ieee} precision: computing it as a
reduction instead changes the summation order, and a rounding-level difference
in a score is enough to change which peer is retained. The per-query kernel
takes $k$ argmax rounds over the score row and reads $k$ value rows, so its
aggregation is sparse and its selection is linear in $k$. The threshold kernel
bisects on the score's integer bit pattern for $34$ steps to find the $k$-th
largest value exactly, then weights every peer by an indicator, so its selection
is independent of $k$ and its aggregation is dense. The sorted kernel maps each
score to an order-preserving integer, packs it against its own peer index into
one $64$-bit word, sorts the row once, and reads the $k$ value rows named by the
top words, so it is independent of $k$ and sparse at the same time. The
pre-score kernel is given the retained indices and never forms a score row at
all: one program per tile of queries walks that tile's retained peers, reading
one key row and one value row per peer and accumulating with a running maximum,
so nothing of size $(B, N, k, d)$ is ever written.

\subsection{Which backend serves the fused dense baseline}

A probe forces each scaled-dot-product backend in turn, at both dtypes and at
three agent counts, under no mask, an additive float mask and a Boolean mask,
and the profiler records which kernel the default dispatcher launches. The
FlashAttention backend is rejected under any non-null mask in either form, and
in \texttt{fp32} under any input, so the library dense baseline runs the
memory-efficient CUTLASS kernel. Forcing FlashAttention on the same unmasked
\texttt{bf16} shape is faster than the kernel we do get, so a baseline able to
reach it would widen rather than narrow the gap we report --- which is part of
why the dense baseline in this paper is the better of the library path and a
kernel we wrote.

\subsection{The host allocator control}
\label{app:cpu}

Dense, masked and gathered each allocate and free several multi-MiB score
matrices per forward. The default allocator returns those pages to the kernel
and they are re-faulted on the next call, which at $N{=}192$ adds roughly
$37{,}000$ minor page faults per forward and inflates latency by up to
$8.8\times$. The tax is proportional to the bytes of large temporaries each
implementation churns, so it differs per implementation and does not cancel in a
ratio; in an un-pinned run it inverted the ordering at one configuration. We
raise the allocator's retention threshold so that freed temporaries are kept,
and count minor faults per forward from the process's own resource usage across
the timed region. The harness records the allocator environment, the thread counts, the affinity
mask and the fault totals in each run's metadata. The fault figures quoted here
come from the host microbenchmark that first exposed the effect, which predates
the re-measurement campaign; the campaign's own artifacts inherit the fix and do
not re-derive the fault counts, so those two numbers should be read as the
diagnosis that motivated the control rather than as a released measurement.

\subsection{Machine balance}
\label{app:roofline}

Peak arithmetic rate is measured with a large matrix multiply and peak bandwidth
with a large copy, in the same process as the measurements they normalise. That
matters here more than it usually would: on this shared host the peak we measure
varies between $58$ and $77$~TFLOP/s across sessions, depending on what else is
running. Every fraction-of-peak figure in the paper is against the peak measured
in the same process as the number it normalises, and is therefore an upper bound
on the true efficiency rather than an estimate of it. Comparisons between two
implementations measured in one process are unaffected; comparisons of a
fraction across sections are not, and we make none. The
copy source is written before it is read: an untouched allocation on this
operating system is backed by the shared zero page, so a host-side copy from it
re-reads one cache line and reports a bandwidth several times the machine's
real figure, and an earlier version of this probe did exactly that: every CPU
ridge point derived from it was too large by roughly a factor of six. Device
work is also timed with device events rather than host wall-clock, since host
timing around a sub-millisecond copy measures launch and synchronisation. The
probe reports two ridge points per device, one with a cache-resident buffer and
one with a buffer larger than the last-level cache, because the two differ by a
factor of two on the host and a claim that holds for one need not hold for the
other.

\section{Additional training analysis}
\label{app:training}

\subsection{Design and the registered comparisons}
\label{app:design}

Twelve variants at up to twelve agents were trained. Besides the communicating
and non-communicating baselines they include fixed-$k$ attention, uniformly
random peers at the same budget, an $\alpha$-entmax normalisation
\citep{peters2019entmax} and a communication budget enforced by a Lagrangian
\citep{stooke2020pidlagrangian} on a per-agent Gumbel-softmax gate
\citep{jang2017gumbel}. Scaling in the number of agents is left to the
training-free measurements of Section~\ref{sec:measurement}, because a training
step in this pipeline is dominated by the environment
(Appendix~\ref{app:backward}). On the partially observable task the fraction of
peers visible falls with the agent count while the absolute number visible
rises, because the world does not grow, so no comparison of returns across agent
counts is made.

The analysis plan fixes the endpoint, six comparisons and their direction, an
exact two-sided permutation test, the effect sizes, a bootstrap interval flagged
approximate at five seeds, and a Holm--Bonferroni correction. It also fixes the
power expectation in advance: at five seeds a first rejection requires one
variant's seeds to lie entirely above the other's, so any null in the family is
under-powered and is not evidence of absence, and no equivalence claim is
available because no equivalence margin was registered.

The primary comparison holds the communication budget, the executed aggregation
cost and every hyperparameter fixed, and varies only whether peers are chosen by
attention score or uniformly at random. Attention-selected minus random peers is
$+4.03$, with a $95\%$ interval of $[-3.35, +11.42]$, a standardised effect of
$+0.60$ and $p = 0.357$. The point estimate favours attention by roughly two
thirds of a standard deviation and the interval includes zero; the design lacked
the power to resolve an effect of that size, as the registered calculation said
in advance, and nothing follows about whether attention selects informatively.

\subsection{Pre-registration chronology}
\label{app:prereg}

The analysis plan was committed when eight of the forty runs in the
pre-registered family had produced a final evaluation and before any of their
returns had been inspected. The timestamped history in the anonymised
supplementary archive establishes the ordering: the
positive-control artifacts at 12:18, the plan at 12:56, and the remaining
artifacts at 19:11 the same day. The plan has two commits, the second appending
to its deviations log; the endpoint, comparisons, test, effect sizes, interval
and correction are byte-identical to the pre-unblinding version. Timestamps
establish ordering and nothing more. Data generation had demonstrably already
begun.

The power statement in Appendix~\ref{app:design} is arithmetic, not an
estimate. With five seeds per variant an exact two-sided permutation test over
$\binom{10}{5} = 252$ assignments has smallest attainable $p$-value
$2/252 = 0.0079$, against a first Holm threshold of $0.05/6 = 0.0083$: a margin
of $3.97\times10^{-4}$. A first rejection therefore requires one variant's five
seeds to lie entirely above the other's, and any effect smaller than complete
separation is undetectable by construction.

One correction is logged against the released plan. It asserts that at most one
comparison in the family can reach corrected significance. That is false: Holm
is a step-down procedure, so once a first rejection is obtained its later
thresholds are looser and further rejections remain possible. The manuscript
states the corrected version; the deviation is logged against the released plan.

\subsection{The remaining comparisons}

Five further comparisons were registered and all are Holm-adjusted nulls. Two
deserve a note. The comparison at twelve agents has the one sign opposite to
its registered direction and carries the family's largest point estimate, and
we set it aside on the ground that neither variant learns the task there. That
ground was not registered: it is a post-hoc exclusion of the largest observed
effect, we have logged it as a deviation against the released plan, and a
reader who rejects it should read the comparison as an under-powered null with
a large point estimate, which is what the registered analysis returns either
way. The evidence for the exclusion is this. A uniform-random policy scores
$-758.9$ under a forty-episode evaluation,
against $-864.9$ for the dense variant, which is worse, and $-717.9$ for the
sparse variant, which is better but barely learning; the comparison is between a
diverged run and a struggling one. The centralised critic sees the same masked
observations as the actors, with no privileged global state, which departs from
standard practice for this learner and is a plausible contributor. Separately, for two comparisons the bootstrap
interval excludes zero while the permutation test does not. The registration
named the permutation test, so it governs; the disagreement is unsurprising at
five seeds, where the interval is coarse, discrete and of uncertain coverage. We
report both so that the estimated effect and the strength of evidence for it are
both visible.

Descriptively, and supporting no claim: the budgeted variant meets its budget
but not by the intended route, its gate collapsing to an expected support near
one within the first tenth of training while the dual multiplier decays to zero;
the entmax variant settles at a support of $1.64$ of five peers; and attention
entropy falls from $0.35$ nats over five peers to $0.12$ over two. The three sit
on different supports and are reported only as an instrumentation check.

\subsection{The partially observable environment}
\label{app:env}

The variant modifies the standard cooperative task in one respect: a peer's
relative-position entry is zeroed when the peer is farther than a fixed radius.
Reward, action space, landmark observations, episode length and observation
dimension are unchanged. Two properties limit what it can support. The mask is a
zeroing rather than an explicit visibility flag, so a peer at relative position
exactly zero is not distinguishable from an absent one. On a continuous position
space that coincidence has probability zero and we did not observe it, but we
did not instrument for it either, and the released characterisation records
visible fractions only. And the visible
fraction falls with the number of agents while the absolute number visible
rises, because the world size is fixed, which is a difficulty confound that
rules out across-agent-count return comparisons.

The communicating variants carry between $7.2\%$ and $19.0\%$ more trainable
parameters than the communication-free baseline, since the attention module adds
parameters the baseline has no analogue for. The positive controls therefore
establish that the communicating architecture outperforms the baseline, not that
the channel alone accounts for the difference.

\section{Reproduction and provenance}
\label{app:repro}

The measurement campaign is training-free and completes in about three hours on
one device, most of it the per-format tiling searches of the precision study.
\texttt{scripts/run\_revision\_campaign.sh} regenerates the fourteen
artifacts every table and figure in this paper is built from, in the order the
manuscript reports them; the equivalence sweep
(\texttt{scripts/verify\_all\_configurations.py}), the launch-cost probe (\texttt{scripts/launch\_overhead.py}), the fixed-set sweep of Section~\ref{sec:fused} (\texttt{scripts/bench\_fixed\_set.py}, about two minutes, which stores every raw sample and times each group eagerly and under CUDA-graph replay) and the backend probe of Appendix~\ref{app:verification} are separate entry points and are named where they are used. Results not produced by the current campaign --- the host-side
allocator diagnosis and the numerical-format robustness checks --- date from an
earlier campaign on the same host, and we say so where they are quoted;
\texttt{scripts/make\_revision\_tables.py} and
\texttt{scripts/make\_revision\_figures.py} regenerate every table and figure
from those artifacts, so no tabular number is transcribed by hand except those of the learning study's Table~\ref{tab:training}, which are copied from \texttt{results/reduced\_design\_summary.md}; \texttt{scripts/check\_revision\_numbers.py} re-derives the numbers that appear in prose and fails on any disagreement; and with \texttt{--verify-paper-numbers} it also checks a manifest (\texttt{scripts/paper\_numbers.py}) that ties each number in the main text, in the section where it appears, to its source artifact, configuration and tolerance. The campaign runs the
tiling search first, because every sweep after it re-times the dense
configuration the search selects; that is what makes each ratio in this paper a
ratio against the best dense implementation we have rather than against
whichever one a given sweep happened to build.

The environment is one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU
(\texttt{sm\_120}) in a shared multi-GPU x86\_64 server, under PyTorch $2.11$
with CUDA $12.8$ and Triton $3.6$; the driver version and the full software
environment are recorded in the supplementary archive. Every latency outside the precision study of
Section~\ref{sec:precision} is \texttt{fp32}, timed with CUDA events
around a single call, interleaved implementation by implementation and
repetition by repetition, with $20$ warm-up repetitions discarded, $40$ to $60$
timed repetitions per block, and the whole block repeated three times (the tiling search instead times each configuration $30$ times after $15$ warm-up calls, in one block, sequentially); the
reported figure is the minimum over all samples and the spread of the three
per-run medians is recorded beside it. The tiling search enumerates query tiles
of $16$ to $128$, key tiles or head chunks of $16$ to $128$, two to eight warps
and one to three pipeline stages, and admits a configuration when its key,
value and accumulator blocks fit a $100$\,kB shared-memory budget; the
precision study repeats that search separately in each format. FlexAttention is
called through \texttt{torch.compile} with the recompilation limit raised and
fallback to the unfused path disabled, since past the default limit it silently
executes an implementation that materialises the score matrix.

Three provenance caveats. The aggregate provenance record predates an upgrade of the
software environment and has been corrected; the run-level provenance for the training variants lives in
the per-run configuration files rather than in the aggregate record. The GPUs
used for every measurement in this paper are shared with other tenants, so
absolute latencies are not reproducible to better than the drift reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:drift} and only ratios between implementations timed in the
same interleaved sweep should be compared. And the activation-memory measurement
is reported from a run with the card otherwise free, since a shared-host repeat
exhausts memory one agent count earlier.
