Sparsification would still be worth having if it cost nothing in return. Testing
that requires care, because on a task where the channel is not needed
``sparsification is free'' and ``the channel was never used'' make identical
predictions, so we gate the study on positive controls and register the analysis
plan before the outcomes are inspected. The study is secondary, and its role is
narrow: to establish that the architecture being sparsified does something, so
that the sparsification comparisons are not vacuous, and to report what a
five-seed design can and cannot resolve. No claim elsewhere in this paper rests
on it.

Twelve variants at up to twelve agents, five seeds each, two million environment
steps, following the protocols of \citet{papoudakis2021epymarl} and
\citet{agarwal2021rliable}, with environments from PettingZoo/MPE
\citep{terry2020pettingzoo} and MAPPO \citep{yu2022mappo} as the learner. The variants include an $\alpha$-entmax normalisation
\citep{peters2019entmax} and a communication budget enforced by a Lagrangian
\citep{stooke2020pidlagrangian} on a per-agent Gumbel-softmax
gate \citep{jang2017gumbel}. Scaling in the number of agents is left to the training-free measurements
of Section~\ref{sec:measurement}, because a training step in this pipeline is
dominated by the environment (Section~\ref{sec:backward}) and cannot attribute a
latency difference to the communication module. Two positive controls establish
that the channel carries something: a reference task in which each agent
observes only its partner's goal, fixed at two agents where selection is
degenerate, and a partially observable variant of a standard cooperative task,
introduced here, in which a peer's relative position is withheld beyond a fixed
radius. In the second, the fraction of peers visible falls with the agent count
while the absolute number visible rises, because the world does not grow; that
confound is why no claim here compares returns across agent counts.

The plan fixes the endpoint, six comparisons and their direction, an exact
two-sided permutation test, the effect sizes, a bootstrap interval flagged
approximate at five seeds, and a Holm--Bonferroni correction. It also fixes the
power expectation in advance: at five seeds a first rejection requires one
variant's seeds to lie entirely above the other's, so any null in the family is
under-powered and is not evidence of absence, and no equivalence claim is
available because no equivalence margin was registered.
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} gives the chronology, the arithmetic behind that
power statement, and the single amendment.

Both positive controls separate completely, each attaining the floor of the
permutation test for this design (Table~\ref{tab:training}), and measured
attention entropy on the partially observable control is $0.35$ nats against a
ceiling of $1.61$, so the learned weighting is peaked rather than uniform.
Sparsification is therefore being applied to an architecture whose communication
machinery improves return under the evaluated setup. It does not follow that the
learned attention channel alone accounts for the difference: the communicating
variants carry $7$--$19\%$ more trainable parameters and the reference task
supplies a discrete utterance channel of its own, so the channel-specific causal
claim would need a parameter-matched sham variant, which we do not have and do
not claim.

The comparison the design exists for holds the communication budget, the
executed aggregation cost and every hyperparameter fixed, and varies only
whether peers are chosen by attention score or uniformly at random. Its point
estimate favours attention by roughly two thirds of a standard deviation and its
interval includes zero. The design lacked the power to resolve an effect of that
size, which the pre-registered calculation said in advance; nothing follows
about whether attention selects informatively. The remaining five comparisons
are in Appendix~\ref{app:training} as point estimates with intervals
\citep{agarwal2021rliable}, and all six are Holm-adjusted nulls.

Two environment limits bound the study further. At twelve agents neither variant
learns the task well --- the dense variant scores below a uniform-random policy
and the sparse variant only just above it --- so no comparison at that agent
count is interpretable; and the centralised critic sees the same masked
observations as the actors, with no privileged global state, which departs from
standard practice for this learner and is a plausible contributor.
